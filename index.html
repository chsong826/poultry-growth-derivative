🐓 Broiler Chicken Study & Data Origin

This simulation is calibrated using live performance data collected from a commercial batch of Ross 308 Broiler Chickens. The chickens were reared in a modern, tunnel-ventilated closed-house system poultry farm located in Gopeng, Perak, Malaysia, operating in compliance with the Department of Veterinary Services (DVS) Malaysia standards.

The math is simple: the function f(x) calculates the cumulative weight of a chicken on day x. The derivative f'(x) gives the instantaneous growth rate, representing the exact number of grams the chicken adds to its body on that specific day. Monitoring this rate allows farm managers to optimize feed transitions and minimize metabolic heat stress.

Chicken Weight Function f(x)
f(x) = A × e-e-B(x - x*)

Calculates total cumulative weight in grams at age x (days).

Daily Gain Rate Derivative f'(x)
f'(x) = A × B × e-B(x - x*) - e-B(x - x*)

Calculates the exact daily growth rate in grams/day on day x.

📍 Malaysian Poultry Science & Farm Management Context

In Malaysia's tropical climate, ambient heat stress is a major impediment to broiler performance. High heat impairs feed intake and triggers systemic inflammation. To combat this, modern Malaysian animal producers utilize closed-house tunnel ventilation with cooling pads to keep the temperature near optimal targets.

Applying growth derivatives helps structure precise feed strategies:

  • Starter Phase (Days 0 to 10): Fast skeletal and gut system division.
  • Grower Phase (Days 11 to 28): Frame and mass acceleration.
  • Finisher Phase (Days 29 to 42): Muscle and fat deposition slows down.
  • Harvest Optimization: Maximum profit window occurs near Day 42.
Reference scientific benchmarks: Malaysian Society of Animal Production (MSAP) guidelines for Ross 308 performance curves under tropical high-humidity housing setups.
